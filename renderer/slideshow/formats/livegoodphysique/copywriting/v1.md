# Copywriting: livegoodphysique v1

## 1. Scope and evidence

This file owns how a selected LIFT CODE message is expressed through language in the `livegoodphysique` slideshow format: list premise, ranking or ordering logic, slide-level verdicts, explanatory density, voice, title, and caption.

- Message meaning and claim limits come from the selected message version.
- Product truth comes from `context/product.md`.
- Reusable strength-training knowledge and content-use limits come from accepted `expertise_entries` retrieved through `context/expertise.md`.
- Each native slideshow project owns its exact slide count, canvas, final media, crops, geometry, typography, colors, and editable layers.
- `context/imagery.md` owns account-wide visual direction and image generation or selection; this format's `imagery.md` owns the reusable visual interpretation of its designated references.
- The three designated posts under `references/post1/`, `post2/`, and `post3/` contain six, eight, and seven ordered slideshow captures. They are execution evidence only; do not copy their wording, rankings, exercise choices, photographs, physique, branding, app interface, or distinctive opinions.
- Captured search bars, arrows, page dots, side controls, black viewer surrounds, and source watermarks are viewer chrome, not format grammar.

The references consistently use a blunt first-person gym-account voice, a clearly bounded `Top 5` premise, and one item per subsequent slide. Some source posts insert an app promotion mid-list. During LIFT CODE's current prelaunch phase, omit product promotion rather than imitating that insert.

## 2. Recurring progression

Use one cover plus one slide per promised item. A `Top 5` premise therefore normally uses six slides. Match the promise exactly; never announce five items and deliver fewer or more.

### Cover

State a narrow, instantly recognizable training decision or judgment.

```text
Top 5 [things/checks/options]
for [specific training stake]
```

- Make the topic understandable without reading the caption.
- Prefer a consequential judgment over a generic educational label.
- Keep the premise narrow enough that every item can be supported and compared on one coherent basis.
- Do not use `best`, `worst`, a numerical score, or an absolute verdict when the admitted evidence does not justify that ranking.

### Item slides

Open with the order marker and a short item label, then add one compact verdict or explanation.

```text
5. [item]
[short practical judgment]
```

or, when a numerical score is genuinely meaningful:

```text
5. [item]
[score]
[short reason]
```

- Descend toward the strongest or most decision-relevant item, normally `5` to `1`.
- Keep one item and one reason per slide.
- A rank may express practical priority for the stated situation; it must not manufacture scientific precision or imply universal superiority.
- Use a score only for an explicitly subjective account judgment or a defined criterion. Do not convert limited evidence into a pseudo-objective `10/10` scale.
- Let the final item deliver the clearest decision rule or highest-value reframe rather than merely the most provocative opinion.
- Preserve exceptions or comparability conditions when omitting them would make the item misleading.

## 3. Voice and density

- Use direct, idiomatic U.S. English.
- Sound like an experienced training partner giving a fast judgment: blunt, compact, physically grounded, and willing to distinguish `useful`, `mid`, and `not enough evidence` without pretending certainty.
- Prefer short concrete lines over paragraphs, citations, or academic framing on the slide.
- Use informal rhythm sparingly. A fragment may carry emphasis, but the underlying claim must remain clear.
- Do not invent personal training history, results, or first-person experience for LIFT CODE.
- Do not use insults, humiliation, body shaming, or contempt for beginners.
- Avoid unsupported `optimal`, `trash`, `guaranteed`, `science-backed`, `everyone`, `never`, and `always` claims.
- If a technical limitation is necessary but too long for the slide, narrow the slide claim and preserve the remaining boundary in the caption.

## 4. Copy hierarchy and density

- The cover uses one compact headline, usually two or three lines.
- Item slides state the rank and label first, followed by a one- or two-sentence reason.
- Keep the visible copy short enough to remain legible at phone size.
- Use line breaks to separate rank, item, and reason. Do not create a dense continuous paragraph.

## 5. Title and caption

- Provide a separate concise post title that states the bounded decision.
- Write one short caption that resolves the list into a practical principle, followed by an evidence/scope note only when needed and a small set of relevant hashtags.
- A save or follow prompt may appear in the caption when it naturally follows from the content; do not add a dedicated CTA slide by default.
- Do not introduce LIFT CODE or planned product capabilities during the current no-product prelaunch phase.
- Name a source only when it is actually admitted and useful for understanding the claim. Do not use a citation list to make a subjective ranking look objective.
