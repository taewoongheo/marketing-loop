## Reference-derived visual grammar

- Follow the reference family's people-first physique photography rather than substituting synthetic-looking atmosphere or equipment-led scenes.
- Prefer real sourced Gym photography over generated people whenever suitable licensed imagery is available. The result should feel like a plausible handheld Gym or social photo, not a polished synthetic campaign render.
- Keep the adult male subject dominant in the frame. The body, pose, and relevant muscle groups must remain immediately recognizable after the final vertical crop; equipment and atmosphere are supporting context.
- Prefer ordinary eye-level, mirror, training-partner, or close three-quarter viewpoints that are physically plausible. Reject cinematic low angles, impossible equipment geometry, ambiguous limbs, hidden physiques, and distant environmental compositions.
- Match the references' direct physique visibility and full-bleed photographic presence without copying baked-in wording, viewer chrome, or a distinctive source composition.
- Treat the natural baseline as subdued color, ordinary mixed Gym lighting, moderate subject distance, visible background complexity, and a subject resting, setting up, or training without acknowledging the camera. Keep exposure, color temperature, lens feel, grain, skin texture, and subject distance coherent across the full sequence.
- Reject advertising and editorial portrait signals even when the photograph is real: heroic monochrome treatment, isolated spotlighting, perfect front-facing poses, cinematic low-angle muscle reveals, immaculate skin retouching, staged brand visibility, or an image that looks cleaner and more art-directed than the surrounding slides.
- Cover, rule, and summary-role slides receive no exemption from the candid visual grammar. Their hierarchy must come from copy placement rather than a campaign-style hero image.
- Prefer images that visibly read as personal training records: a mirror selfie, a phone set up during an exercise, or an imperfect frame taken by a nearby friend. Ordinary ceiling lights, reflections, clutter, companions, partial occlusion, and uneven framing are positive evidence when the person and action remain understandable. A professionally lit photograph that merely imitates candid body language is still outside this format's grammar.
