## Reference-derived visual grammar

- Retain the reference family's Japanese-manga figure attention device without importing a franchise identity, creating an app character, or letting character appeal replace the strength-training decision.
- Use anonymous, one-off, clearly adult male manga figures as secondary narrative cues or embodied reactions rather than as branded characters or the source of a training claim. Figures may change across slides; do not imply character continuity or make one figure a recurring account mascot.
- A figure may support an attention, explanation, example, or scope slide and may be absent from evidence-heavy or final slides. Character presence is not a required slot.
- Keep slide backgrounds solid and quiet so long explanatory copy remains immediately legible. Do not force manga figures into photographic Gym scenes or detailed environmental backgrounds.
- Preserve `restrained wildness` through a composed or serious expression, purposeful posture, dark or desaturated Gym-adjacent clothing, hard controlled contrast, and at most one restrained signal color.
- Keep the figure subordinate to the copy through controlled screen occupancy, edge crops, silhouettes, or partial/full-body poses; the slide must still read as disciplined strength-training content rather than a character poster.
- Let each pose embody one copy-relevant state such as uncertainty, checking, restraint, effort, or resolution. Do not use decorative poses unrelated to the slide's decision.
- Exclude child-coded proportions, chibi treatment, exaggerated bashfulness or cheerfulness, romantic or school-life staging, sexualized framing, collectible-character polish, and ornate fantasy costume.
- Do not reproduce or closely evoke a recognizable character's face, hair, outfit, pose, accessories, palette, setting, or franchise symbols. Reproduce only broad manga conventions such as monochrome linework, screentone, decisive silhouettes, and emotionally legible posture.
