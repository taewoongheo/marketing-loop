# trainmystyle Copywriting v1

## Scope

This version governs English copy for `slideshow/trainmystyle`. The format turns one bounded muscle-region or equipment-constrained session into an immediately scannable anatomy-led carousel. It owns wording roles and progression, not exercise truth, project geometry, or image-generation policy.

Use product truth and accepted `expertise_entries` for every exercise, dosage, effort, or outcome claim. During prelaunch, deliver standalone training value and do not mention or promote LIFT CODE as an app.

## Reference-derived language grammar

The designated references use a direct promise on the cover, followed by one exercise card per slide. Their useful transfer is the speed of comprehension: one region or session, one movement name, one visual demonstration, and one compact prescription. Do not copy their exact topics, wording, distinctive expressions, illustrations, or branding.

## Cover

- Make one concrete promise that names the training region or session and the operative constraint.
- Prefer a two-line structure that can be understood before the supporting image is inspected.
- Keep the hook practical and literal. Do not add a curiosity preamble, motivational setup, or unsupported outcome guarantee.
- Name available equipment when it is central to the usefulness of the carousel.
- Do not add an eyebrow, category pill, sequence counter, or other label when the promise already communicates the constraint.

## Exercise slides

Each exercise slide uses exactly two language roles:

1. **Movement name:** the precise, unambiguous exercise name supported by the shown setup.
2. **Prescription line:** one concise set-and-repetition or effort instruction supported by accepted evidence and qualified when needed.

Rules:

- Use the same role order across the carousel.
- Keep movement names concrete; include setup qualifiers such as `Chest-Supported` or `Seated` when omitting them would change the exercise.
- Keep each role visually scannable rather than writing explanatory sentences over the demonstration.
- Do not add slide counters, target-muscle pills, category chips, or decorative micro-labels. The title, highlighted anatomy, and prescription already carry the necessary information.
- Use one shared prescription only when it is defensible for every listed movement. Otherwise write movement-specific prescriptions.
- Do not call alternatives equivalent, identical, or one-to-one replacements.
- Do not promise hypertrophy or strength from one routine, prescribe one universal optimum, or imply that every set must reach momentary failure.

## Progression and density

- Use one cover plus four to six exercise slides.
- Lead the sequence with the highest-priority broad movement, then alternate or group movements only when the order remains coherent as a session.
- Preserve a near-reference density: one dominant title, one clear visual demonstration, and one dominant prescription line per exercise slide.
- Do not add coaching paragraphs, numbered technique steps, or a final recap slide. Put necessary limitations in the caption.

## Voice

- Direct, competent, and calm.
- Use familiar gym language, but retain exact exercise names.
- Prefer short imperatives and concrete nouns over hype.
- Avoid `ultimate`, `perfect`, `best`, `must`, `guaranteed`, and transformation claims.
- Title case is allowed for movement names.

## Brand adaptation

Preserve the references' white-space-heavy anatomy-card clarity while replacing their branding with restrained LIFT CODE visual cues. The copy itself remains standalone training value. Do not insert an app reveal or CTA during prelaunch.

## Post title and caption

- **Post title:** restate the session and equipment constraint in plain language; do not introduce a second promise.
- **Caption:** explain who the session is for, add the narrowest evidence-required qualification for effort, load, rest, or dosage, and end with a low-friction save prompt.
- Keep hashtags specific to strength training, the trained region, and the equipment. Avoid broad engagement bait.

## Adaptation reasoning

The references earn attention by removing interpretation cost: the viewer sees the movement, highlighted anatomy, and prescription at once. LIFT CODE should keep that immediate utility while improving precision through unambiguous exercise names, cautious dosage language, and a cleaner evidence boundary. The format should feel like a usable session card, not a compressed article or an app advertisement.
