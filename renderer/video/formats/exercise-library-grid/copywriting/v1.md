## 2. Format identity and eligibility

### Discovery job

Use this format when the useful audience question is:

```text
What exercise options fit this stated body area, movement family, equipment condition, or training constraint?
```

Choose one organizing scope per content. Valid scope types may include:

- one body area with a clearly bounded exercise set;
- one movement family;
- one available equipment condition;
- one setup or angle distinction;
- another specific exercise-discovery question supported by admitted expertise.

Do not combine unrelated body areas, equipment conditions, and training purposes merely to fill the grid.

### Not a routine

A simultaneous catalog has no implied performance order.

- Do not call the grid a `routine`, `workout`, `program`, `session`, or `day` when no supported sequence and prescription are provided.
- Do not imply that every displayed option should be performed together.
- Do not use position in the grid as an exercise ranking, recommended order, or priority unless the approved copy explicitly and defensibly establishes that meaning.
- Use `routine-sequence` when the viewer should perform an intentional ordered prescription.
- Use `workoutclock` when several simultaneous demonstrations together form a compact, actionable exercise group or routine board rather than a discovery catalog.
- Use `exercise-alternatives` when the viewer must compare an unavailable exercise with a practical alternative under a specific constraint.

### Cohesion

Every item must answer the same opening promise.

- Define whether the set is representative, selected, or exhaustive. Default to a bounded selection rather than implying completeness.
- Include materially distinct options rather than several visually similar variations whose useful difference cannot be identified.
- State the equipment condition when it changes what qualifies for the grid.
- Reject an item that cannot be accurately named, visually distinguished, or supported within the selected scope.

## 3. Copy flow

### Opening channel

State the organizing scope before the viewer attempts to identify individual demonstrations.

```text
[body area, movement family, or training purpose]
[exercise options, variations, or library]
[optional equipment or setup constraint]
```

- Prefer a concrete scope such as `Dumbbell back exercises` over a broad promise such as `Build your entire back`.
- Use `library` only for a discoverable option set, not to imply that every possible exercise is included.
- Use `complete`, `all`, `every`, `best`, or a number-based completeness claim only when the evidence and actual selection support it.
- Do not frame a catalog as a guaranteed muscle-building prescription.
- Keep the opening short enough that it does not obscure the exercise demonstrations.

A label-only opening is preferred. Use one short sentence-level hook only when the discovery condition would otherwise be unclear.

### Grid-item identification

Every displayed option must map to an exact exercise name. An unnumbered, unlabeled animation grid is not sufficiently actionable for LIFT CODE.

Choose one identification system:

1. **Direct labels:** place one short exercise name with each demonstration.
2. **Numbered mapping:** place a stable number with each demonstration and provide the matching ordered name list in a legible on-screen legend or the caption.
3. **Staged legend:** preserve the simultaneous demonstrations while presenting bounded groups of matching names in a clearly traceable order.

Possible language roles are:

```text
[number]
[exercise name]
[optional equipment or setup distinction]
```

```text
1. [exercise name]
2. [exercise name]
3. [exercise name]
```

- Use the precise, idiomatic U.S.-English exercise name supported by admitted expertise.
- Keep numbering and reading order stable throughout the video.
- Keep exercise-name grammar parallel.
- Shorten a name only when the shortened form still identifies the intended variation accurately.
- If exact names cannot remain legible at the selected item count, reduce the item count or use a traceable legend rather than shrinking text past readability.
- Do not force the viewer to infer equipment, body position, grip, or exact variation from a small animation when that distinction matters.
- Do not attach sets and reps to catalog items by default. A prescription changes the audience job from discovery toward programming.

### Target and distinction channel

A target label or muscle overlay may clarify why an option belongs in the selected scope.

- Use a target claim only when its meaning, evidence, and limitations are admitted.
- A shared highlight does not prove identical stimulus, interchangeability, equal effectiveness, or complete muscle coverage.
- When two similar options appear, identify the decision-relevant distinction rather than relying only on different images.
- Do not use anatomical color as decoration that implies unsupported precision.

### Grid rhythm

The reference supports several simultaneous looping demonstrations held long enough for visual scanning.

- Keep the grid useful without a product insert or external explanation.
- Give each item enough visible motion to distinguish the exercise family or setup.
- Use only as many items as remain identifiable and coherent.
- The reference's nine-item arrangement is evidence of a dense catalog, not a required count or reusable layout.
- Do not convert a visual scanning grid into an ordered sequence through arbitrary animation timing.
- Exact item count, geometry, clip phase, loop duration, title timing, and transition belong to the native project rather than this copywriting version.

### Spoken text

The reference contains no audio. Default `spoken_text` to an empty channel.

Use spoken text only when a necessary selection boundary cannot remain intelligible through the title, labels, legend, and caption. Spoken text must clarify the discovery set rather than narrate every visible movement.

## 4. Claim and decision boundaries

- Trace every exercise name, inclusion decision, target statement, and distinction to admitted expertise before using it.
- Present the grid as options under a bounded scope, not as personalized programming.
- Do not imply that all displayed options should be performed in one session.
- Do not infer completeness or exercise quality from the number of tiles.
- Do not rank exercises by grid position, visual size, animation intensity, or target-overlay area.
- Do not use `best`, `must-do`, `only exercises you need`, or universal superiority language without direct support.
- Do not treat visually similar target overlays as evidence that exercises are substitutes.
- Do not use this compact discovery format for pain, injury, medical limitation, or rehabilitation selection.
- Select another format when the audience needs form coaching, programming rationale, or a multi-condition decision process.

## 5. Writing

### Voice and rhythm

- Use idiomatic U.S. English.
- Sound controlled, practical, precise, and physically grounded rather than clinical, motivational, or gym-bro theatrical.
- Prefer concrete words such as row, pulldown, dumbbell, barbell, cable, machine, bench, grip, and angle.
- Use parallel exercise names and concise scope labels.
- Avoid `ultimate`, `perfect`, `secret`, `hack`, guaranteed-result language, and unsupported `science says` authority.
- Do not use the word `CODE` as a hacker or software metaphor.
- Run a separate exercise-name, item-mapping, scope-consistency, and idiomatic-English pass before showing final copy.

### Information limit

This format is for immediate option discovery, not layered explanation.

- One short discovery scope is the maximum sentence-level argument.
- The remaining language identifies exercise options and necessary distinctions.
- Put one global equipment or scope boundary in the opening or caption instead of repeating it in every tile.
- Do not make the caption carry an explanation that reverses or materially qualifies an unsupported grid.

## 6. Ending, CTA, title, and caption

During the current prelaunch phase, end on the useful exercise grid rather than the source product screen.

- Default to no dedicated on-screen product reveal or download CTA.
- A short save-oriented action may appear in the caption when the grid is independently useful.
- Provide a separate concise post title with every final copy. The title is not part of the caption.
- When numbered mapping is used, preserve the exact grid order in the caption list.
- Compose the caption with one concise description, the required ordered exercise names or one important equipment or scope boundary, and a small set of relevant hashtags.
- Do not use the caption to repair an inaccurate exercise identity or unsupported selection after publication.
- Do not introduce product promotion during the current no-product prelaunch phase.
